Our Lineup

The Courses

Four rounds on Nos. 2, 4, 5 and 10, plus a loop around The Cradle.

Four rounds and a bonus loop: the U.S. Open course, Doak’s sand-mine course, Hanse’s sandy redesign and old-school No. 5, then The Cradle for fun. Here’s what we’re walking into.

No. 2

“Four U.S. Opens, turtleback greens and no rough.”
History

Donald Ross opened it in 1907 and kept tinkering with it for decades. It has hosted four men’s U.S. Opens: Payne Stewart in 1999, Michael Campbell in 2005, Martin Kaymer in 2014 and Bryson DeChambeau in 2024. Payne’s statue stands next to the 18th green.

Style

Coore & Crenshaw’s restoration took out the rough and brought back the hardpan sand, wiregrass and native scrub along the fairways. The greens are crowned “turtlebacks” that throw anything a little off line down the slope.

Difficulty

7,588 yards and par 70 from the tips, with a 76.5 rating and 138 slope. Johnny Miller said hitting these greens is like landing a ball on top of a VW Beetle.

Inside Info

Aim for the middle of every green and live with a long putt. From just off the green, the putter is often the smart play, because a chip that doesn’t clear the crown rolls back to your feet.

No. 10

“Tom Doak, a sand mine and a lot of room.”
History

Pinehurst bought 900 acres of old sand-mining land about five miles south of the village, where The Pit Golf Links used to be. Tom Doak designed the course, his associate Angela Moser ran construction, and it opened in April 2024. Sports Illustrated named it Best New Course of 2024.

Style

Hilly ground with huge leftover mining mounds. The 8th plays between them, including one called the “Matterhorn.” Expect rugged built-up bunkers, native scrub, and sloped greens split by sideways tiers.

Difficulty

7,020 yards and par 70 from the tips, with a 74.1 rating and 142 slope, the highest slope of our four. Think of the 12th and 13th as one par 9.

Inside Info

Use the ground. Slopes around the first two greens feed low approaches onto the putting surface. On the 634-yard par-5 10th, cross bunkers about 100 yards short make laying up the usual play. Station 21 does a breakfast buffet right by the first tee.

No. 4

“Sandy wastes and dramatic views.”
History

Ross laid out the first version in 1919. Robert Trent Jones, Rees Jones and Tom Fazio all redid it over the years. Gil Hanse rebuilt it in 2018, ripping out the rough and Fazio’s pot bunkers. It shared stroke play with No. 2 at the 2019 U.S. Amateur.

Style

Native sand and wiregrass instead of rough, wide corridors and long views across the property. Pinehurst sums it up as “sandy wastes” and “dramatic vistas.”

Difficulty

7,227 yards and par 72 from the tips, with a 74.9 rating and 138 slope. The longest of our four, but the most forgiving off the tee.

Inside Info

A miss into the sand usually leaves a clean lie on hardpan, so don’t panic. The 2nd is a short downhill par 5 at 473 yards and an early birdie chance. The 460-yard 5th is the one to survive.

No. 5

“Tall pines and more water than anywhere else at Pinehurst.”
History

Ellis Maples, who learned course design under Donald Ross, built it in 1961. Robert Trent Jones reworked it in 1974.

Style

A classic parkland feel compared with the sandy courses, with towering pines and more water in play than any other Pinehurst course. The par-3 14th, framed by a pond and tall pines, is known as the Cathedral Hole.

Difficulty

6,823 yards and par 72, with a 135 slope. The shortest of our four, but the water makes up for it.

Inside Info

Bring extra balls for the water and have the camera ready at the Cathedral Hole. With tall pines lining the fairways, a straight drive matters more here than on the sandy courses.

The Cradle

“The most fun 10 acres in golf.”
History

Gil Hanse built it in 2017 on a small patch of ground just south of the main clubhouse, next to the Thistle Dhu putting course.

Style

Nine par 3s from 56 to 127 yards, 789 yards in all. Pinehurst lists the atmosphere as “music/barefoot,” so the speakers are on and shoes are optional.

Difficulty

Par 27. A wedge, maybe a 9-iron, and a putter. The hard part is your touch after a couple of drinks.

Inside Info

It works best as an afternoon loop after a morning round, or on arrival day. Play it as a contest: closest to the pin on every hole.
